For the first weeks of my Nepali YouTube channel, most of my Shorts were explainers with an AI voice. Clean visuals, a list of points, a voice that never stumbles. They were easy to make in bulk.

Then I started posting short clips of myself, talking to the camera in my room. Same channel, same week.

This is what happened.

## The numbers

These are public YouTube Shorts on my Nepali channel, published between 12 and 19 November 2026, with views counted on 20 November.

| Format | Shorts | Total views | Average views | Likes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Me talking to camera | 4 | 1,326 | 332 | 28 |
| AI voice-over explainers | 8 | 248 | 31 | 4 |

On average, a talking Short got about ten times the views of an AI voice-over Short.

The four talking Shorts:

- **612 views.** "12 जनाको team लाई AI ले १ जना बनायो... त्यो १ जना म थिएँ" (A team of 12 became 1 because of AI... I was that 1). 41 seconds. People watched 57% of it on average.
- **321 views.** "AI ले राम्रो उत्तर दिँदैन? प्रायः समस्या प्रश्नमा हुन्छ" (AI does not give good answers? Usually the problem is the question). 38 seconds. It got these views in under one day.
- **198 views.** "+2 मा biology, doctor बन्ने सपना। आज software developer।" (Biology in +2, a dream of becoming a doctor. Today a software developer.) 30 seconds.
- **195 views.** "एक वर्ष भयो, हातले एक line पनि code लेखेको छैन" (One year, and I have not hand-written a single line of code).

The eight AI voice-over Shorts got between 15 and 77 views each. The lowest ones were list-style episodes: "5 skills outside the syllabus" got 15, a one-minute Nepal Decoded episode on how many dollars people earn from Upwork and Fiverr in Nepal got 16, and an episode on teaching AI your own documents got 18.

## Please read this part: the sample is small

I want to be clear about what this is and what it is not.

- **4 talking Shorts and 8 voice-over Shorts.** That is very few. One lucky video can change an average of four.
- **One week, one channel.** This is my Nepali channel only, in one week of November.
- **The biggest one pulls the average up.** The 612-view Short alone is almost half of the talking-head total. Without it, the other three average 238. That is still far above 31, but less dramatic.
- **The sort is by title.** The weekly numbers check grouped videos by their titles ("Ep" in the title means a voice-over episode). Nobody opened every file to confirm the format.
- **Topic is mixed with format.** My talking Shorts were about my own story and a common AI pain. My voice-over Shorts were mostly lists and explainers. So I cannot say if people liked my face, or my story, or both.

So this is a lean, not proof. But a 10x gap is too big for me to ignore.

## Why I think it happened (my guesses)

These are guesses. I have not tested them one by one.

**A person beats a voice.** An AI voice can read a perfect script. It cannot say "this happened to me". The 612-view Short is me saying that my client's team went from 12 people to just me, and then AI replaced me too. That is not a fact you can look up. Only I can say it.

**Pain and story beat lists.** The top talking Shorts were either my own story, or one direct problem with one direct answer. The lowest voice-over Shorts were lists with an "Ep" number in the title. A list asks the viewer to stay for five points. A story asks them to find out what happened.

**Shorter did better here.** The top talking Shorts were 30 to 41 seconds. The one-minute voice-over episode got 16 views. Again, small sample.

## It also showed up outside YouTube

On my Nepali channel overall, YouTube views from 13 to 17 November were 913, compared with 519 the week before. Almost all of that came from two days: 400 views on 16 November and 409 on 17 November. The 12-to-1 talking Short went up on 16 November.

I cannot prove that one Short caused those two days. But the timing matches.

## What I am changing

Here is what I am doing because of these numbers.

1. **Talking Shorts go first on the Nepali channel.** I changed the order of this week's shoot because of these numbers. The first-person clips come first: my first freelance job, my first day, how I started in IT. These are closest to what worked. The explainer-style clips come last.
2. **Fewer AI voice-over list episodes in the main slot.** I am not deleting the format. But numbered list episodes stop being the main daily Nepali post.
3. **Two finished voice-over Shorts are dropped.** They were ready to post. One was a Git commands list, and it was already public with 30 views. The other was a prompting list. My 321-view talking Short already covers the prompting idea better. Posting them anyway would just fill a slot.
4. **English gets the same test.** On my English channel, I have no talking Short in public yet. The AI voice-over episodes there average 67 views. So the first English talking Shorts are a fresh test. I will post one, read the numbers, and only then decide on the rest.
5. **I keep measuring.** Next week I will compare again with more videos. If the gap gets smaller with a bigger sample, I will say so here.

## What I am not concluding

I am not saying AI voice-over is dead, or that every creator should switch to talking head. I still use AI every day to write, edit and build these videos. The voice-over format is fast, and for some topics it may still be the right tool.

What I am saying is smaller: on my Nepali channel, this week, people watched me much more than they watched an AI voice. As someone whose whole story is about being replaced by AI, I find that a little funny. The part of the work AI could not replace on this channel was the part where I show up and say what happened to me.

If you are making Shorts with AI voice-over and the views are stuck, try a few clips of yourself talking. Keep everything else the same if you can. Then compare your own numbers, not mine.
