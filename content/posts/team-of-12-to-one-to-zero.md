In my last job, I watched a team of about 12 people become a team of one. I was the one.

Then the one became zero. That was me too.

I am writing this down plainly, because I see two kinds of stories about AI and jobs. One says AI will make you rich. The other says AI will take everything from you. My story is neither. It is just what happened.

## How I got there

I did +2 in biology. The plan was medicine. Then Covid came, and the plan changed. I chose IT, studied for a degree here in Nepal, and got my first own laptop at the start of my bachelor's.

I started freelancing in my first year: WordPress sites first, then HTML, CSS and JavaScript, then PHP, Python and Django. Landing pages, lead forms, whatever paid.

About six months after graduating, a friend and I started a software company. I was the lead developer, and later the team lead for about 15 people. I also did the accounts, the bills and the office work, because in a small company someone has to.

## The main client

For more than two years, most of our work came from one client: a US-based marketing agency that worked with lenders.

I built a lot for them. WordPress and Django systems first. Then automation in GoHighLevel, a CRM: workflows, templates copied across many sub-accounts, and an app on its API. Tools that delivered leads. Tools that made personalised AI-avatar videos for campaigns. A subscription panel.

In the beginning, I took over another developer's code, and I worked 16 to 17 hours a day to understand it and keep it running. That is how I learned the system so well.

## From 12 to 1

Over those two years, the client kept making their own team smaller. Not in one day. Slowly. Every time AI could do a part of the work, that part did not need a person anymore.

Their team was about 12 people. At the end, it was just me.

I was the one they kept. I knew the system, and I was using AI in my own work too.

## From 1 to 0

Then they let me go too, as AI took over the rest.

That client was our main income. When it went, our company shrank to three working people. I left in the middle of August 2026.

It was a business decision. AI got good enough at the work, and the client did not need to pay a person for it anymore.

## What I learned: I was already doing the new job

Here is the strange part. By the time I was let go, I had already changed how I work.

I have not hand-written code for almost a year. AI writes it. I review it, test it, and decide what goes in.

That sounds lazy when you say it fast. It is not. My day is:

- **Deciding what to build** and in what order.
- **Writing clear instructions**: the rules, the context, what "done" means.
- **Reading what the AI wrote**, and asking why when something looks wrong.
- **Testing**, because AI is confident even when it is wrong.
- **Saying no**: to extra features, to clever code nobody asked for, to changes that touch things they should not touch.

The typing part got cheap. The judging part did not.

## The travel site in 7 days

The clearest example for me is a travel website I built with Claude in 7 days.

It is not a toy page. It has a Next.js front end, a Django back end with an admin dashboard, an AI trip planner that answers questions about the trips, and the SEO basics like a sitemap and robots file. Today it runs as a portfolio demo with a made-up brand.

I asked myself what my old team would have quoted for the same thing. My honest guess is about a year.

I do not say this to show off. I say it because it explains what happened to my job. If one person with AI can build in a week what a team used to quote in a year, then the client does not need the team. My guess is that the same logic, one step further, is what ended my part too.

## What I am not saying

I am not saying "AI will take your job". I do not know your job. I only know mine.

I am also not saying "learn AI and you will be fine". I did learn it. I used it every day. It did not save that job.

What I think, from my own case only:

- **The work that is easy to describe goes first.** If your task can be written as clear steps, AI will do those steps.
- **Judgment lasts longer.** Knowing what to build, what to remove, and when the AI is wrong is still a human job, for now.
- **Being the last one is not a plan.** I was the last one on that team. That felt safe. It was not.

## Where I am now

I am back in my own house, in a village near Panauti. Not because I had to. I chose it.

I am building a content business on my own: a Nepali tech channel, an English AI and dev channel, and this blog. I built a system called Studio that runs my YouTube, Facebook and Instagram posting, also with AI writing the code and me deciding.
